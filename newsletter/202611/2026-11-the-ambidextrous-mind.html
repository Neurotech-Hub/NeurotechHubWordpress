The Ambidextrous Mind

The Neurotech Hub Monthly  ·  November 2026

I recently heard Uber’s CEO describe great leaders as ambidextrous: equally capable of leading at scale and solving the problem right in front of them.

Great scientists and engineers share this ability. They know when to zoom out, when to zoom in, and how to move fluidly between the two.

As AI makes once-difficult technical problems increasingly tractable, that fluency may be our greatest advantage.

Knowing which questions to ask. Connecting ideas across disciplines. And understanding the people we’re solving problems for.

This month, I’m sharing a few tools and collaborations that help us do just that.

HubTracker

Our internal command center

HubTracker is our database for every person, project, hour of effort and dollar. From one place, we generate invoices, track project revenue and expenses, log effort, schedule equipment, and deconflict shared space. New this fall: unique analytics tags for any web tool we build, and an internal API so our bots can drop in data and produce time-bound reports.

Collaborations

Amplifying our Impact

A few collaborations where Hub engineering is taking science somewhere new.

A Key Engineering Role in ARPA-H SPECTRA

The Neurotech Hub is collaborating with Dr. Rebecca Schwarzlose on a proposed study exploring whether early sensory experiences and sleep patterns can help identify emerging autistic traits during infancy. Our engineering team will develop a miniature, low-power wearable that tracks environmental light, sound levels, and location throughout an infant’s first year of life, balancing precision sensing with comfort, battery life, and data privacy.

The project brings together neuroscience, engineering, and design to explore new opportunities for earlier identification and support.

Spatial Foraging Module Gets a User-Friendly Interface
